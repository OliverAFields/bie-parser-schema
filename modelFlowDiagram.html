BCR–TLR9–NF-κB: how one cell responds

This is the biology encoded in the model's CSV modules. Tonic B-cell receptor signalling and a CpG/TLR9 pulse converge on one shared TAK1 pool. TAK1 switches on IKK, and IKK frees NF-κB to enter the nucleus. Nuclear NF-κB then induces its own inhibitors (IκBα, IκBε, A20), each after a fixed transcriptional lag.

What each simulated cell goes through

  1. Resting phase (1,000 h). Only the tonic BCR signal is on and there's no CpG. The cell settles to its own baseline of TAK1, IKK and NF-κB.
  2. CpG pulse (24 h). CpG rises smoothly to height H at time t*, then slowly decays. NF-κB enters the nucleus, and its feedback genes then pull it back out.
  3. Readouts. Nuclear RelA:p50 and total IκBα (all forms summed), averaged over 15 cells.

Why the cells differ

  • Each cell gets its own CpG timing t* (median about 25 min, up to 12 h) and height H. Around 1 in 6 cells get H = 0 and don't respond.
  • Rate constants flagged distribute are scaled by about ±11% per cell.
  • M-CLL vs U-CLL differ only in tonic BCR input (0 vs 0.005), so the conditions differ in their baseline before the CpG pulse arrives.
